# Silver fine-tuning of TrOCR: results (27 Nov 2026)

**Question.** Does continuing TrOCR A on the reviewed v3.0 silver pool improve handwriting recognition? How do the supplied silver transcripts (B) compare with filtered teacher transcripts (C)?

**Answer on validation.** Not on its own. Training on silver alone helps the three sources silver covers but forgets dictation, which silver does not contain. B gains significantly on archive, school and university and loses on dictation, so overall it is not better than A. C, trained on A's own confident transcripts, is worse than A on every source. Adding human replay to B's data (exploratory run **M**) keeps the silver gains without the dictation loss. M reaches line CER **0.1046 against A's 0.1150** (−0.0104, paired 95% interval −0.0145 to −0.0065). It also beats a step-matched human-only continuation (H) by 0.0085. The extra steps alone explain little of the gain.

All numbers below are on the original v2.2 human validation split: 133 pages, 2,076 eligible annotated lines, beam 4, as in notebook 03. **The v3.0 test set was not touched.** Full tables, the training curves and the teacher filter are in [silver/README.md](silver/README.md), generated by `scripts/silver_report.py`.

## Runs

All runs start from A's saved weights (`models/trocr-uk-baseline`) with a fresh AdamW optimizer. They share A's recipe: lr 3e-5, weight decay 0.01, linear schedule with 10% warm-up, batch 16 and seed 42. Each run takes exactly **2,800 optimizer steps**, about three passes over B's lines. Targets over 128 tokens are dropped, as for A. Every 400 steps the checkpoint is scored with greedy decoding on validation, and the lowest greedy CER is kept, as A's epoch was chosen.

| Run | Training data | Distinct lines (archive / school / university / dictation) | Passes |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| **A** | human v2.2 train (midterm baseline) | – | – |
| **B** | silver, supplied transcripts (`target`) | 14,918 (7,183 / 3,876 / 3,859 / 0) | 3.0 |
| **C** | silver, frozen-A transcripts after the declared filter | 8,572 (4,198 / 2,362 / 2,012 / 0) | 5.2 |
| **H** | human v2.2 train again (optional control from the plan) | 19,204 | 2.3 |
| **M** | B's silver lines + human v2.2 train, shuffled together *(exploratory, designed after B/C were scored)* | 34,122 | 1.3 |

**C's filter** was fixed before C was trained and never reads the supplied silver text. Accepted lines have 4–140 characters, at least one Cyrillic letter, no word repeated three times in a row, and A's mean token log-probability of its own output at or above τ = −0.061. τ is the lowest threshold at which A's accepted validation lines reach CER ≤ 5%. On the silver pool A's transcripts differ from the supplied ones by CER 0.158: 0.082 on accepted lines and 0.246 on rejected ones.

## Findings

1. **The supplied silver transcripts carry real signal.** B beats A on every silver source. The largest gain is on university, the weakest source at midterm: 0.199 → 0.171.
2. **Silver-only training forgets dictation.** B's dictation CER rises from 0.078 to 0.115. Its worst dictation lines are tall, multi-line crops (median aspect 6.1 against 13.9 for all dictation). On them B transcribes a *neighbouring* line. Silver boxes are 20–30% taller than human boxes from the same source, so B adapts to a different box convention. Silver labels are not shifted between lines: in only 13 of 14,918 crops does A's reading match a neighbouring transcript better than the crop's own.
3. **Self-training (C) does not help.** C fits A's own outputs (training loss 0.006 at the end) and drifts. It is worse than A everywhere and worse than B on the silver sources. Confidence filtering removes bad labels but adds no new information.
4. **Human replay fixes the forgetting (M).** M matches B on the silver sources (difference not significant) and A or H on dictation, and is significantly better than both A and H overall. Two M runs with different data orders gave 0.1061 (stopped at step 2400) and 0.1046 (full run), so the gain is stable.
5. **Restarting the optimizer costs accuracy at first.** Every run, H included, gets worse at step 400 (greedy CER 0.14–0.16 against A's 0.121) when warm-up brings the learning rate back to 3e-5, and then recovers. Every run's best checkpoint is its last, so a lower peak lr or a longer schedule may help further.

## Caveats

- **M is post hoc.** It was designed after B and C were scored on validation, so its validation gain is slightly optimistic. The pre-declared B/C comparison is unchanged. M needs the frozen test comparison before the team adopts it.
- Checkpoint selection and reporting both use validation, as for A. The test set has the last word.
- B and C use torch's seeded shuffling. H and M were trained after GPU hangs began and use a seeded, resumable batch order instead (recorded per run in `train_*.json`). Only H's final run and M's rerun were complete. A stalled M run, scored at its step 2400, gave a consistent 0.1061.
- Rescoring A here gives 0.1150 against 0.1145 in notebook 03 (bf16 and batch effects). Page CER for A reproduces notebook 09 exactly (0.1849).
- 292 silver targets (2%) still contain braces. `clean_text` misses `~~old~~ {new}` when a space separates the two parts, as in `Право {участі у} виборах…`. B and M were trained on these targets as supplied.
- `silver/teacher_silver.csv` contains the supplied silver transcripts next to A's outputs. `data/silver.md` says only IDs are shared, so decide whether to commit it.

## Reproduce

```sh
nohup setsid scripts/run_silver_htr.sh > results/htr/silver/log_driver.txt 2>&1 &   # about 5 h on the RTX 5060 Laptop
uv run python scripts/silver_report.py                                              # tables -> results/htr/silver/README.md
```

The runner skips finished stages, kills and retries a stage that stays silent for 7 minutes, and resumes H and M training from their last evaluation. The GPU in this WSL2 laptop occasionally hangs without raising an error. Checkpoints (`models/trocr-uk-silver-B`, `-C`, `trocr-uk-human-H`, `trocr-uk-silver-human-M`) stay outside Git.
